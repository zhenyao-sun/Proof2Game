Write \(d=n-1\), \(p=p_c\), \(q=p/2\), and \(L(R)=\log(2+R)\). The extension bound is for every \(g\in L^p(S^{n-1},d\sigma)\), and the tube bound is for every \(f\in L^n(\mathbb R^n)\); each bound is uniform over these inputs. Apply \ref{ms:thm-1-2} with a smooth radial annular cutoff equal to one near the unit sphere. Its power \(A=2/p\) in \ref{ms:lemma-9-2} gives the first bound.

For \(0<\delta\le1/2\), take a maximal \(\delta\)-separated set \(\{\omega_j\}\) in \(S^{n-1}\). Each average defining \(K_\delta f(\omega)\) is at most a constant times the corresponding \(2\delta\)-tube average in any direction \(\omega_j\) within \(\delta\) of \(\omega\). Indeed the first tube lies in the second, and each tube has volume comparable to \(\delta^d\). The averages are continuous in center and direction by continuity of translation and rotation of tube indicators in \(L^q\), so their supremum is measurable. The covering by caps of radius \(\delta\) gives
\[
 \|K_\delta f\|_{L^n(S^{n-1})}
 \lesssim\left(\delta^d\sum_j K_{2\delta}f(\omega_j)^n\right)^{1/n}.
\]
Test this discrete norm by \(a_j\ge0\) with \(\delta^d\sum_j a_j^q=1\). For any centers \(x_j\), put \(T_j=T_{2\delta}(\omega_j,x_j)\). Hölder's inequality and \ref{ms:lemma-9-4} give
\[
 \delta^d\sum_j \frac{a_j}{|T_j|}\int_{T_j}|f|
 \lesssim\int |f|\sum_j a_j\mathbf 1_{T_j}
 \lesssim L(1/\delta)^{2A}\|f\|_n.
\]
Take the suprema over centers and weights. For \(1/2\le\delta\le1\), Hölder's inequality on each tube gives the bound.

Let \(F\) contain a unit segment in every direction. Its open \(\delta\)-neighborhood contains the tubes around those segments, so \(1\lesssim L(1/\delta)^{2A}|F_\delta|^{1/n}\). To prove the Hausdorff assertion use ball covers, grouping radii as in \cite{r-44}, Claim 3.2. For a countable cover by balls \(B_j\) of radii \(r_j\le e^{-2^m}\), put
\[
 J_k=\{j:2^k\le\log(1/r_j)<2^{k+1}\},\quad
 \delta_k=e^{-2^{k+1}},\quad G_k=\bigcup_{j\in J_k}2B_j,\quad v_k=\sum_{j\in J_k}r_j^n.
\]
At a point on a given unit segment covered by a ball in \(J_k\), its transverse disk of radius \(\delta_k\) lies in that doubled ball. Disks at different points form disjoint cross-sections of the tube. Hence the length covered by group \(k\) is at most a dimensional constant times \(K_{\delta_k}\mathbf1_{G_k}\) in the direction of the segment. All groups together cover length one in every direction. The triangle inequality, the maximal estimate, and Hölder in \(k\) give for any \(\gamma>n-1\)
\[
 1\lesssim \sum_{k\ge m}2^{2Ak}v_k^{1/n}
 \lesssim \left(\sum_j h_\gamma(r_j)\right)^{1/n}
 \left(\sum_{k\ge m}k^{-\gamma/(n-1)}\right)^{(n-1)/n},
\quad h_\gamma(r)=r^n[\log(1/r)]^{2An}[\log\log(1/r)]^\gamma .
\]
The last factor tends to zero. The gauge is doubling for small radii, so the ball covers give infinite Hausdorff measure for \(h_\gamma\). Since \(h_\gamma(r)=o(r^s)\) for every \(s<n\), the dimension is \(n\).
