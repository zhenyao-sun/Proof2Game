For $m=1$ the cofactor is identically one. Otherwise let $j_0=m-1$. By Lemma~\ref{ms:lemma-3-2}, $C$ is homogeneous of degree $j_0$. Polarize it by taking $Q(M_1,\ldots,M_{j_0})$ to be the coefficient of $t_1\cdots t_{j_0}$ in $C(\sum_jt_jM_j)$. This is multilinear and equals $j_0!C(M)$ on the diagonal $M_1=\cdots=M_{j_0}=M$.

Take $M_j=g_jg_j^*$ for quaternionic columns $g_j$. If all $t_j>0$, the kernel of $\sum_jt_jg_jg_j^*$ is the orthogonal complement of the span of the $g_j$, as seen by setting its nonnegative quadratic form equal to zero. Its image is that span, of dimension at most $m-1$. Its cofactor therefore kills every $g_j$ by Lemma~\ref{ms:lemma-3-2}. The equalities are polynomial in $t$, so the coefficient $Q(g_1g_1^*,\ldots,g_{j_0}g_{j_0}^*)$ also kills every $g_j$. This coefficient is Hermitian, so $g_j^*Q=0$ as well.

Evaluate the polarized row divergence on $u_j(z)=\exp(\xi_j\cdot z)$ with arbitrary real covectors $\xi_j$ and the real coordinate dot product. Write $(g_j)_a=\sum_\alpha e_\alpha(\xi_j)_{a\alpha}$. The Hessian symbol in Lemma~\ref{ms:lemma-3-1} gives
\[
Q(H(u_1),\ldots,H(u_{j_0}))
=e^{(\sum_j\xi_j)\cdot z}Q(g_1g_1^*,\ldots,g_{j_0}g_{j_0}^*).
\]
After dividing by the exponential, the row divergence is $(\sum_jg_j)^*Q=0$. On general smooth inputs this row divergence is a multilinear constant-coefficient differential expression. Its exponential test divided by the product of the exponentials is the polynomial whose monomials in the $\xi_j$ encode the derivatives of the respective inputs. It vanishes for all real covectors, so all its coefficients vanish. Taking every input equal to $u$ and dividing by $j_0!$ gives the assertion.
