Solving the definitions of $Y,W,\Phi$ gives the stated inverse. Its denominator is bounded away from zero on $|W|\le w_0$, and $|aQ|\le a/10<s/10$ gives $|Y|\asymp s$. The formulas thus give inverse holomorphic maps on neighborhoods of the closed fibers and depend smoothly on $\tau$.

Set $F=(W/w_0,10Q)$ and let $J$ be its complex Jacobian in $(\tau,P,Z)$, with spatial submatrix $J_{\rm fib}$. Since $r=|F|^2-1$, Lemma~\ref{ms:lemma-3-1} gives $H(r)=4J^*J$ and $G(r)=2J^*F\mathbf i$. The matrix $J_{\rm fib}$ is invertible. In the full space the complex direction acquired by varying $\tau$ at fixed $(W,Q)$ spans $\ker J$ over $\mathbb C_{\mathbf k}$. Its quaternionic span is the full kernel of $H(r)$, as the rank of $J$ over $\mathbb H$ is $n$ by the invertible spatial submatrix. This kernel is orthogonal to $G(r)\in\operatorname{im}J^*$ and is contained in the kernel of $H(|\Phi|^2)$, since the last row of $J$ is a nonzero multiple of $\partial\Phi$. In the inverse chart the derivatives of $(P,Z)$ in $W$ are $O(s)$ and those in $Q$ are $O(a)$. This gives $\|J_{\rm fib}^{-1}\|\lesssim s$ and the lower Hessian bound. For the gradient bound, use $|F|\le1$ to get
\[
  G_{\rm fib}(r)G_{\rm fib}(r)^*=4J_{\rm fib}^*FF^*J_{\rm fib}
     \le4J_{\rm fib}^*J_{\rm fib}.
\]

At fixed $\tau$ the complex Jacobian determinant of the inverse chart is $\sqrt 2\,aY^d/(1+W\cdot W/2)$. Indeed the exterior product of the $d$ differentials of $P=WY$ with $\mathrm dY$ leaves only $Y^d$ times the $d$ differentials of $W$ wedged with $\mathrm dY$, while $\mathrm dZ=\sqrt 2\,\mathrm dY$ and $\partial_Q Y=a/(1+W\cdot W/2)$. Squaring its absolute value gives the volume estimate. The quaternionic determinant of $H_{\rm fib}(r)$ is $4^n|\det_{\mathbb C}J_{\rm fib}|^2$, since the positive complex Hermitian matrix $J_{\rm fib}^*J_{\rm fib}$ has the same eigenvalues as a quaternionic Hermitian matrix. This proves the determinant estimate. The spatial derivatives of $\Phi$ are bounded and $\partial_Z\Phi=(1-W\cdot W/2)/\sqrt2$ is bounded away from zero, which proves the rank and eigenvalue assertion by the holomorphic Hessian formula. All first derivatives of $W=P/Y$ are $O(s^{-1})$, so the bound on $|G(|W|^2)|$ follows.
